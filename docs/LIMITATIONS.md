# Limitations

What this project does not do, or has not been shown to do.

## Detection quality

- **Accuracy is unmeasured.** No labelled dataset ships; thresholds were tuned on synthetic fixtures. Use `services.calibration_cli` on your own labelled media to get real numbers ([how](USER_GUIDE.md#measure-real-accuracy)).
- **Heuristics, not a classifier.** Apart from the optional image backbone you train yourself, every AI-versus-real decision is hand-written threshold logic over pixel, spectral and metadata signals.
- **"PRNU" is a median-filter noise residual**, not a camera fingerprint.
- **Faces are found with YuNet**, a published CNN detector bundled in `core/models/`. Counts looked right on a manual review of 36 personal photos (including one 33 px background face), but recall and precision on your media are not measured. The facial *deepfake-risk* score on top of it is still a texture heuristic.
- **The face-authenticity model is only as general as its training set.** It was trained on one Kaggle dataset (about 5,000 real faces, 4,600 AI faces) and scores 100 % on that dataset's held-out images, which says the dataset is easy, not that the model is. How it behaves on faces from other generators (Midjourney, Flux, face swaps, beautify filters) is unmeasured. On 44 faces from 36 real phone photos it called none AI-like, which checks false alarms only. The first version of this model learned the dataset's framing instead of faces and called every AI face real when run through the real pipeline; it was retrained on crops built exactly as at inference, and the training report now includes an inference-path accuracy.
- **Recognition is zero-shot.** Place, species and vehicle type are the best match inside a fixed vocabulary, so they always return something; results below 30 % are shown as "not sure". Rare species and specific vehicle models are weak spots.
- **Expression recognition is indicative.** The model reaches about 88 % on a posed-face benchmark; real photos (stern group poses, sunglasses, side views) are harder, and "disgust" in particular is over-reported.
- **Same-person matching is within one photo set only**: nothing is stored, no names are attached. It is accurate on frontal faces and weak on small, blurred or turned faces.
- **Attribution is a guess.** Only some generators have spectral calibration; the rest match metadata strings. Attribution never changes the verdict.
- **C2PA is presence-only**; signatures and hash bindings are not verified. **EXIF is unauthenticated.**
- **Learning is shallow.** Feedback nudges a few scalar weights; real model training is a separate, manual step. Near-duplicate files (re-saves, resizes) are not recognised as the same sample.

## Specification versus implementation

The three `GLOBAL_*_TAXONOMY_AND_FORENSIC_RESEARCH_REPORT.md` files describe a very broad target. Each ends with an implementation-status appendix. Dimensions marked spec-only (for example astrophysical, subatomic or event-sensor imagery, or zero-knowledge media proofs) are research scope: recognised or documented but not scored, and no claim of coverage is made for them.

## Operations

- **Not tested on GPU.** The CUDA code path exists but has never been run; the tests cover CPU behaviour.
- **CI has never run on GitHub.** The workflow is written and its commands pass locally.
- **No authentication.** Per-session scratch folders keep users' files apart, but the app is for personal, local use only.
- **Batch analysis is sequential.** The pipelines share models and were not designed for parallel runs.
- **Size limits:** image 100 MB, audio 200 MB or 1 hour, video 500 MB.

## Age and minor screening (`core/perception/age.py`)
- **No model finds every child.** MiVOLO v2 (Apache-2.0) is the strongest open age model found in the October 2026 research, with a mean error of about 3.7 years on its own benchmark and 4-5 years elsewhere, but a 2026 independent benchmark of 34 models found that off-the-shelf age models mislabel many minors as adults when the cut-off is exactly 18, and all of them are weakest under about 5 years old. The screening is therefore recall-first and sends uncertain people to a human (`review_required`).
- **The margin was measured, and it is a trade-off.** On 2,250 UTKFace faces with known ages (`python -m services.age_eval`), flagging "estimated age below 27, or the age-group opinion at least 50 % child/teenager" sends 99.2 % of under-18s to review (97.7 % of 16-17 year-olds) but also 87 % of 18-20, 75 % of 21-25 and 26 % of 26-35 year-olds. A plain "age < 18" rule finds only 85.6 % of minors (46.7 % of 16-17). Those faces are pre-cropped and well lit, with one face per image and no body; recall on real photographs, with small faces, occlusion, filters and make-up, will be lower and has not been measured. UTKFace itself is licensed for non-commercial research only.
- **Faces and bodies must be visible.** People whose face and body are both too small, cut off or hidden come back `UNDETERMINED`. Drawings, cartoons and AI-generated people are aged by appearance, which was not validated for them.
- **Apparent age, not real age.** Make-up, filters, lighting and image quality move the estimate; the result says nothing about a person's actual age or identity.
- **Weights.** `core/models/mivolo_v2/model.safetensors` is the upstream file at revision `53393526c220e34cdd7b722b36d22b6f9e5f4241` (115 MB, stored with Git LFS). The network code in `core/perception/mivolo_vendor/` is vendored from the MiVOLO repository (Apache-2.0, licence included).
- **Real photographs, measured.** On 750 LAGENDA photographs (CC BY 2.0; group scenes, small faces) with the real detectors, 99.6 % of 722 annotated minors were sent to review (3 recorded as missed), and all 69 minors with faces under 40 px were found. Looking at the 3: two were scoring errors (the child was flagged but the evaluation paired them with the adult beside them; the matcher is fixed in `services/age_eval.py`, the run has not been repeated), and one is a 13-year-old whose face is rotated 90 degrees, which the face finder cannot see (the body-only fallback still flagged it). The 99.6 % is therefore approximate: matching errors can move it either way. 36.9 % of annotated adults also went to review. MiVOLO v2 was trained on LAGENDA, so its age accuracy there is optimistic; on FairFace, which it never saw, the 0-2 and 3-9 groups were flagged 99.0 % and 99.5 % (`python -m services.age_eval`, datasets in docs/AGE_DATASETS.md). This is annotator-labelled Open Images data, not a sample of your media.
- **Small faces.** The screening uses its own recall-first face finder (`core/perception/face_scan.py`: lower confidence, enlarged overlapping tiles). In pasted-face tests a child's face reached review 85 % of the time at 20 px, 100 % at 28 px and 62 % at 16 px, and 30 % at 14 px; below about 15 px nothing is reliable. The price is that about 8 % of photographs with no people (animals) are sent to review because the finder sees a face-like pattern.
- **Rotated faces.** OpenCV applies a photograph's EXIF orientation when it loads it, so phone portraits are upright; but a sideways or upside-down face in pixels with no orientation tag is invisible to the upright face finder. On 200 LAGENDA photographs (256 annotated faces) the upright finder found 95.9 % of annotated minors' faces; scanning the whole image rotated added only 1.5 points (97.4 %) at a cost of about 1 second per photograph and a false rotated "face" in 10 % of people-free photographs, so that is not done. Instead, a person detected without an upright face is searched, inside their own box only, for a face turned 90, 180 or 270 degrees (`AgeEstimator.assess(scan_rotated=True)`). If one is found the person is aged both ways and the younger age and the larger child share are kept, so a false face can only add a review. The sideways 13-year-old that had been a body-only POSSIBLE_MINOR (21.7) became LIKELY_MINOR (16.7); on 30 control photographs (167 people) no verdict changed and the time per photograph stayed at 4.0 seconds. A sideways face on a person the person detector also misses is still not seen.
- **Video.** `core/perception/video_sampling.py` + `age_video.screen_video_file` choose frames adaptively: the frames after every content change (cut, or someone entering part of the shot) plus a grid of at most one a second, up to 60 frames; a frame that looks like the last examined one reuses its result. One flagged frame sends the whole video to review (priority REPEATED when it recurs, SINGLE_FRAME otherwise), and the result reports the longest unexamined stretch (`sampling.longest_unexamined_seconds`; 2 s on the test clips). Measured on 15 synthetic clips (real LAGENDA children, at least 150 px faces, spliced into sea-animal footage): a child on screen for 0.5-3 s was flagged in 13 of 13 clips, against 5 of 13 with the old 16 even frames; the 2 child-free controls were not flagged by either. Clips over about a minute exceed the 60-frame budget, so the grid thins (a 10-minute clip examines roughly one frame every 10 s) and shorter appearances can be missed. Children in the background or in small insets rely on the same image-level age model, so the face-size limits above apply. There is no tracking, so the same child in ten frames counts as ten sightings. Only synthetic clips were tested; no real video set was measured.

## AI-versus-real verdicts on photographs
- **False "AI" on real photos is reduced, not gone.** On 600 real colourful animal photos (Fish, Nudibranchs, Corals, Puffers) the AI verdict fell from 22-49 % of each folder in the first blind test to 5-12 %; 54 of the 600 are still called AI, mostly by the CGI / digital-art route (25) and by pixel evidence (23). Whale and Shark photos fell from about 88 % to 3-7 %.
- **The digital-art weight change had no measurable effect.** Running the old and the new weight on the same 664 photos gave identical verdicts: the earlier hue and small-image fixes did the work. The new weight is kept because a likelihood ratio cannot exceed 1 / P(finding | real) and the finding fires on 4-20 % of real photographs.
- **Wallpapers (no ground truth)** stay at about 61 % AI.
- **Video clips, measured.** Twelve synthetic 60-second clips (real photographs, a child on screen for 0, 1, 2, 4, 8 or 15 seconds, run through the real frame sampler): every examined frame that showed a child was flagged, the two clips with no child were clear, and 9 of the 10 clips with a child were sent to review. The one miss (a 2-second appearance) fell between two sampled frames. With 16 frames over 60 seconds the spacing is about 4 seconds, so the chance of seeing a child is about min(1, seconds on screen / spacing): roughly 25 % for 1 second, 50 % for 2 seconds and certain from 4 seconds, and the 10 clips here were luckier than that. The clips test sampling and aggregation, not motion blur, compression or fast cuts. Screening a clip takes 11-31 seconds.
- **Two metadata and noise false positives fixed.** (1) The generic AI-generator match was a plain substring search, so the camera field `aux:ImageNumber` matched "imagen" and added a large AI score to 66 Sea Animals photos, 54 in the Celebrity folder and one wallpaper; it is now whole-word, and the ambiguous short names (gemini, flux, runway, sora, pika, luma, kling, imagen) count only in software-naming fields. (2) The "uneven local noise" (inpainting) finding fires on 6-20 % of real small photographs but on 4 % of AI faces, so it is not counted for images of 400 px or less; on large camera photographs it fires on 0.4 %. Held-out AI faces are unchanged (300 of 300 still AI).
- **Your "Family" folder is not all camera captures.** It holds two `Gemini_Generated_Image_*.png` files (credited "Made with Google AI") and one Topaz Photo AI enhanced photograph, which the detector correctly reports as AI-related.
