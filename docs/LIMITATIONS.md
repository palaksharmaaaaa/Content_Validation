# Limitations

What this project does not do, or has not been shown to do. Numbers below were measured on the datasets named; none is a guarantee for your media.

## Detection quality

- **Overall accuracy is unmeasured.** No labelled AI-versus-real dataset ships; thresholds were tuned on synthetic fixtures. Use `services.calibration_cli` on your own labelled media to get real numbers ([how](USER_GUIDE.md#measure-real-accuracy)).
- **Heuristics, not a classifier.** Apart from the optional face check and the optional image, video and audio backbones you train yourself (none ships), every AI-versus-real decision is hand-written threshold logic over pixel, spectral, temporal and metadata signals.
- **"PRNU" is a median-filter noise residual**, not a camera fingerprint.
- **Faces are found with YuNet**, a published CNN detector bundled in `core/models/`. Its recall and precision are not measured here. The facial *deepfake-risk* score on top of it is a texture heuristic.
- **The face-authenticity check is optional and no model ships.** Without a checkpoint the Faces check reports that it is not calibrated and adds nothing to the score. If you train one (`python -m image_detector.face_training`), it is only as general as your training set: a classifier trained on one dataset's real and generated faces can score near-perfectly on that dataset's own held-out part and still fail on other generators, face swaps or beautify filters, so measure it on faces it never saw. The starting weights are ImageNet-1K ResNet-18 (non-commercial research terms) and the licence of your faces applies (see THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md).
- **Recognition is zero-shot.** Place, species and vehicle type are the best match inside a fixed vocabulary, so they always return something; results below 30 % are shown as "not sure". Rare species and specific vehicle models are weak spots.
- **Expression recognition is indicative.** The publisher reports about 88 % on a posed-face benchmark (not re-checked here); real photographs (stern group poses, sunglasses, side views) are harder.
- **Same-person matching works within one photo set only**: nothing is stored and no names are attached. It is expected to be reliable on frontal faces and weak on small, blurred or turned faces; it has not been measured here.
- **A generator is named only when the file itself declares it** (a visible watermark, or a metadata or software claim, all unauthenticated). Spectral slope, motion variance, cutoff frequency and canvas size are shared by many generators and by ordinary cameras and codecs, so they rank candidates but never name one; without a declaration the answer is "Unknown" and no candidates are offered. A file name is never used. Attribution never changes the verdict.
- **C2PA is presence-only**; signatures and hash bindings are not verified, and a bare four-letter tag counts only next to the JUMBF box type. **EXIF is unauthenticated.**
- **A cue that could not be measured is not a calm reading.** Fewer than three frame differences, fewer than three frames for flicker, an unreadable EXIF field or an unmeasurable spectrum are reported as "not measured" (`None`) and take no part in the score.
- **A file name is not evidence.** Nothing in the verdict, the screenshot decision or the attribution reads it.
- **Blank or degenerate media get "no usable content", not a verdict**: a single flat colour, silence, all-blank video frames. Pictures and videos whose shorter side is under 64 px are rejected with the reason.
- **Learning is shallow.** Feedback nudges a few scalar weights, and only from values that were actually measured for that file; real model training is a separate, manual step. The same file (identical bytes) with the same answer is counted once, so repeating a submission cannot push the thresholds further; near-duplicates (re-saves, resizes) are still treated as different samples, and anyone who can submit feedback can still skew the thresholds with many distinct files.
- **No confidence band above "leaning".** `core/bands.py` keeps `HIGH_CONFIDENCE_ENABLED = False`: until the scores are calibrated on labelled media, no result is labelled "high confidence", however extreme the number.
- **Hard JPEG compression hides grain.** A JPEG saved below libjpeg quality 95 (or with unrecognised tables) has lost fine grain whatever made it, so noise, smoothness and spectral-slope readings are not counted towards AI for it; they may still count towards real. Images under 512 px on the short side are not scored on spectral slope. Both rules were set from synthetic re-save experiments, not a labelled corpus.
- **A block list that cannot be used switches the gate off visibly.** A missing, empty, unreadable or unparseable hash list leaves the gate `INACTIVE` with a note; it never reports `CLEAR`.
- **The out-of-distribution gate needs at least as many reference files as features** (and at least 20); with fewer it stays uncalibrated rather than flagging ordinary files.
- **Text taken from a file is escaped before display** (`ui/text.py`) and spreadsheet formulas are neutralised in CSV exports, but this is checked only by unit tests and AppTest, not by a browser-level review.
- **Open items.**
  - Feedback is deduplicated by file content and label, each correction moves the thresholds less than the one before (the steps shrink as 1 / (1 + n/10)^2, so their total is bounded and volume alone cannot reach a clamp), and every value is clamped and validated on load. One kind of mistaken correction can still shift a threshold by up to that bound, the Learning tab shows what changed, and "Reset thresholds to defaults" (per type, behind a confirmation box) sets the learned file aside (renamed, not deleted) so everything the corrections taught can be undone in one click.
  - The script/HTML signature needs a real tag (`<script`, `<html` followed by a space, `/` or `>`, or `<?php `), so prose in metadata no longer matches; a picture that really embeds such a tag is still flagged, and the finding is advisory only.
  - Golden tests compare digests of whole outputs: they detect any change but do not say what is right. Accuracy on real AI media is unmeasured (see the first bullet).
  - Video frames are opened by OpenCV only from local files in a supported container (MP4/MOV, MKV/WebM, AVI): a link is downloaded by the hardened fetcher first, and a playlist named like a video is refused before OpenCV sees it.
- **What is not covered at all** is listed in [CHECKS.md](CHECKS.md#not-covered).

## Operations

- **Platforms.** Tested on Windows locally and on Linux (Python 3.14) by CI on every push. macOS has never been run.
- **GPU.** The CUDA path exists but has never been run; the tests cover CPU behaviour.
- **Scale.** Nothing tests sustained heavy load. Analysis is CPU-bound; see [Performance](../README.md#performance) for measured times. Concurrent analyses are safe (six at once gave results identical to sequential runs) but share one set of models in one process.
- **No authentication, by design.** The app is a self-contained tool and ships with no sign-in. It listens on 127.0.0.1 by default. Per-session scratch folders keep uploads apart, but every user of one installation shares the same learned corrections, the same Learning tab (file names and notes included) and the same feedback copies; put your own access control in front of it if you serve it to other people.
- **Batches run in parallel on multi-core machines** (images and audio about 2x faster, video about 1.15x); on a dual-core machine they stay sequential.
- **Size limits** (`limits.toml`, or `OMNIFORENSICS_MAX_<IMAGE|VIDEO|AUDIO>_<FILE|BATCH>_MB`): one file up to 10 MB for an image, 100 MB for a video (and 1 hour), 10 MB for audio (and 1 hour); one batch up to 500 MB per type. Each uploader hands its per-file limit to Streamlit, so the browser refuses a bigger file at once; restart the app after changing `limits.toml`.
- **`ffmpeg` is called with only the `file` and `pipe` protocols, only the container formats the app supports (`-format_whitelist`), no terminal input and a bounded decode.** An upload that is really a playlist, concat list, HLS or DASH manifest is refused instead of being followed to other files. The program is found by an explicit `PATH` search, never in the current folder.
- **Without `ffmpeg`**, WAV audio (8, 16, 24 and 32-bit PCM) is still read natively and video frames are still read through OpenCV, but compressed audio cannot be decoded and a video's audio track is skipped.

## Age and minor screening

Code: `core/perception/age.py`, `age_video.py`, `video_sampling.py`, `face_scan.py`. Evaluation tools: `python -m services.age_eval`; datasets in [AGE_DATASETS.md](AGE_DATASETS.md).

- **No model finds every child.** MiVOLO v2 (Apache-2.0) is the strongest open age model found in the October 2026 research, with a mean error of about 3.7 years on its own benchmark and 4-5 years elsewhere. A 2026 independent benchmark of 34 models found that off-the-shelf age models mislabel many minors as adults when the cut-off is exactly 18, and all are weakest under about 5 years old. The screening is therefore recall-first and sends uncertain people to a human (`review_required`).
- **The margin is a recall-first design choice, not a measured result.** Flagging "estimated age below 27, or the age-group opinion at least 50 % child or teenager" is meant to send nearly every minor to review at the price of also sending many young adults; a plain "age < 18" rule would miss the minors the model takes for adults. How many of each are caught or flagged has not been measured on any data kept in this repository: run `python -m services.age_eval` on your own faces with known ages (UTKFace is licensed for non-commercial research only).
- **Faces and bodies must be visible.** People whose face and body are both too small, cut off or hidden come back `UNDETERMINED`. Drawings, cartoons and AI-generated people are aged by appearance, which was not validated for them.
- **Apparent age, not real age.** Make-up, filters, lighting and image quality move the estimate; the result says nothing about a person's actual age or identity.
- **Weights.** `core/models/mivolo_v2/model.safetensors` is the upstream file at revision `53393526c220e34cdd7b722b36d22b6f9e5f4241` (115 MB, Git LFS). The network code in `core/perception/mivolo_vendor/` is vendored from the MiVOLO repository (Apache-2.0, licence included).
- **Real photographs.** Group scenes and small faces are harder than clean crops. `services.age_eval wild` scores the whole chain on annotated photographs (for example LAGENDA, CC BY 2.0); no such run is kept here. MiVOLO v2's publisher does not say what it was trained on, so an evaluation on a public set may overstate its accuracy if that set was in its training data.
- **Small faces.** The screening uses its own recall-first face finder (lower confidence, enlarged overlapping tiles), so small faces are found more often than by the ordinary finder, but below roughly 15 px nothing is reliable. The price is that some photographs with no people (animals) are sent to review because the finder sees a face-like pattern.
- **Rotated faces.** OpenCV applies a photograph's EXIF orientation on loading, so phone portraits are upright, but a sideways or upside-down face with no orientation tag is invisible to the upright face finder. Scanning every whole image rotated costs about a second per photograph and adds false faces, so it is not done. Instead, a person detected without an upright face is searched, inside their own box only, for a face turned 90, 180 or 270 degrees; the younger age and larger child share of the two readings are kept, so a false face can only add a review. A sideways face on a person the person detector also misses is still not seen.
- **Video.** Frames are chosen adaptively: the frames after every content change (a cut, or someone entering part of the shot) plus a grid of at most one a second, up to 60 frames; a frame that looks like the last examined one reuses its result. One flagged frame sends the whole video to review (`REPEATED` when it recurs in at least two frames and a quarter of those examined, otherwise `SINGLE_FRAME`), and the result reports the longest unexamined stretch. Clips longer than about a minute exceed the 60-frame budget, so the grid thins (a 10-minute clip gets about one frame every 10 s) and shorter appearances can be missed. There is no tracking: the same child in ten frames counts as ten sightings. Video screening has not been evaluated on any real video set.

## AI-versus-real verdicts on photographs

- **False "AI" on real photographs is possible.** Colourful or heavily processed real photographs, digital art and CGI can be called AI by the digital-art route or by pixel evidence. The rules were adjusted to reduce this (a hue guard for natural single-colour scenes, no inpainting finding on small images, grain evidence ignored on hard-compressed JPEGs), but the rate on real photographs has not been measured here.
- **The digital-art finding is capped.** A likelihood ratio cannot exceed 1 / P(finding | real), and the finding can fire on real photographs, so its weight is kept small.
- **Two metadata and noise false positives were fixed.** (1) The generator match was a plain substring search, so the camera field `aux:ImageNumber` matched "imagen"; it is now whole-word, and ambiguous short names (gemini, flux, runway, sora, pika, luma, kling, imagen) count only in software-naming fields. (2) The "uneven local noise" (inpainting) finding fires on ordinary small photographs, so it is not counted for images of 400 px or less.
- **Some real files really are AI-related.** Photographs credited "Made with Google AI" or enhanced with Topaz Photo AI are correctly reported as AI-related.
